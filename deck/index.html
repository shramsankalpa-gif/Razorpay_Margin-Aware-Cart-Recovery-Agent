The problem · Recover and Grow with AI

Cart recovery today buys back orders, not profit

Abandoned-checkout tools are set up and judged on orders recovered. The discount goes to every shopper who comes back, including those who would have bought anyway, so recovery can quietly erode the merchant's margin.

Why this is a Track 2 problem

  • Recover: abandoned carts are a large, recurring revenue leak. Baymard's average cart abandonment is about 70%.
  • Grow: the prize isn't more orders. It's more margin kept on the orders recovered.
  • AI-native: the right discount depends on who the shopper is, how they pay, when they shop and what's in the cart. No fixed rule can capture that.

Merchant: a D2C brand on Magic Checkout

  • Several categories with very different margins, e.g. serums 72% vs protein 42%
  • Sends a flat or tiered coupon to abandoned carts and reads success as recovered orders
  • Has no way to see which discounts created a sale and which gave margin away

How recovery works today

Shopper leaves checkout

Before paying, or after a failed UPI, card or OTP attempt.

Abandonment event fires

Magic Checkout's abandoned-cart webhook, or an agent watching for it.

A preset offer goes out

Picked from the merchant's preset coupons (flat, tiered by cart value), never above the merchant's cap.

Success = orders recovered

No public tool measures what would have happened without the discount.

What Razorpay offers publicly (Oct 2026)

  • Magic Checkout coupons are merchant-made: flat, %, tiered, BXGY, with cart-value conditions.
  • Agent Studio cart agent picks among offers the merchant set up, never above the merchant's maximum.
  • None of this public material describes a control group, margin floor or incremental-profit report.

Profit = orders × AOV × (margin − discount). At 10%, a third more orders earn zero extra profit: the 150 who'd have paid anyway now get ₹100 each.

Conversion dashboards report 10% as the winner. The merchant's P&L doesn't.
Problem: recovery offers are chosen from a preset menu and judged on orders won back, so merchants pay discounts to shoppers who'd have returned anyway and can't see the margin they lose.
Opportunity: use the checkout signals Razorpay already sees to price each abandoned cart's offer for profit kept (often ₹0), and prove the gain against a reminder-only control group.
The checkout journey · where profit is made and lost

Maximise profit across both paths to purchase, not conversions

328–360 carts

No profit, whatever the discount. Shoppers who left over delivery dates, trust or just browsing don't come back for a bigger coupon.

Two objectives

Today maximise orders: α + (1−α)·c(d) → rises with every extra % of discount
This agent maximise profit kept
α·m  +  (1−α)·c(d)·(m − d)
α·m
profit from shoppers who pay straight through. Offers only go out after abandonment, so d can't change it.
(1−α)·c(d)·(m − d)
abandoned share × share who come back at d × margin left after d. This is the only part the agent controls.

For each abandoned cart, the agent tries every d from 0 to the cap and keeps the highest c(d | x)·(m − d). A bigger d raises c but shrinks (m − d), and the best point is often ₹0.

Notation

When does a discount pay? Only if (c(d) − c(0))·(m − d) > c(0)·d: extra orders × margin left must beat the discount handed to shoppers who'd have returned anyway. Flat 10% here: 2.1 vs 2.0, so it barely pays.

α checkout conversion rate (pay without abandoning) · c(d) recovery conversion rate at discount d; c(0) with a reminder only · m gross margin, as a share of selling price · d discount, as a share of eligible items' price · x the cart's signals.

The solution · an Agent Studio agent

Margin-Aware Cart Recovery prices every abandoned cart's offer for profit

STEP 1

Trigger

Abandoned checkout or failed payment (Magic Checkout event + gateway result)

STEP 2

Scope

Cart has ≥1 of the merchant's top-5 margin categories; otherwise the normal flow runs

STEP 3

Read signals

10 checkout signals plus the categories in the cart

STEP 4

Predict

Chance of purchase c(d | x) at every discount from 0 to the cap, in 0.5% steps

STEP 5

Decide

Pick d* for maximum expected profit within cap, floor and blocked groups; or assign to test or control

STEP 6

Message

“Save ₹75” on eligible items only. Failed payment → retry link first

STEP 7

Learn

Outcome logged, model re-fit, control group measures the lift

Why Magic Checkout is the trigger: it is Razorpay's own D2C checkout, so at drop-off it already holds the cart, the payment attempt and its failure reason, the consented contact and the UTM source, and it emits an abandoned-cart event. No new tracking is needed. Why first-time vs repeat: repeat buyers already trust the brand and convert about 2× more on a reminder alone, so a discount mostly leaks margin there.

What the merchant sets

  • Top 5 categories by margin. Only carts containing one are in scope, and only those items are discounted. Low-margin items have no room to discount.
  • For each category: a product type (shared taxonomy), a margin % (private, never pooled) and a max discount %.
  • A minimum margin after discount, the channel (WhatsApp or email), and review before going live.
  • Entered as a form or uploaded as Excel or PDF. The AI extracts the values and shows a diff to confirm.

How it learns from outcomes

  • 10% control: reminder only, always on. This is the yardstick for every profit claim.
  • Random test offers from 0 to the cap in 0.5% steps. Randomised offers are what make the discount effect causal, not just correlated.
  • Explore → learn → optimise: 100% → 45% → 12% of carts keep testing (a bandit in production).
  • New merchants don't start from zero: Razorpay's existing checkout data, pooled by product type across live merchants, gives day-one curves, and the store's own carts refine them. Margins and shopper data are never pooled.
  • Shoppers see a rupee saving rounded to ₹5, so odd percentages don't look arbitrary.

Review · change · approve · stop

  • Review first: any change to limits, signals or groups waits for Approve and go live.
  • Change: caps, margin floor and categories, plus a switch per signal that shows its profit cost (region tier adds only about ₹0.4–1k per 1,000 carts).
  • Override: set any group to reminder only, e.g. all COD carts.
  • Understand: "what the agent learned" in plain words, a "Why?" per group, and the top drivers per cart.
  • Why the merchant keeps the upper limit: pricing and the P&L are theirs, and they know what the model can't (stock, brand positioning, sale calendar). The cap bounds the worst case and makes the agent explainable. Agent Studio's own rule is that agents never exceed the merchant's maximum.
  • Stop: pause the agent in one switch. Every cart keeps an audit log of offer, reason and checks.

Business outcome measured

North star

Incremental profit per 1,000 abandoned carts = treated profit − control profit, after discounts

  • Efficiency: discount ₹ per incremental order; share of offers at ₹0
  • Volume: recovery rate vs control
  • Guardrails: opt-out and complaint rate, margin-floor breaches (must be 0), returns on recovered orders
  • Adoption: merchants who finish margin setup; share of recommendations approved
